    Worker environment · live

    Monitoring temperature, air pressure & combustible gas at each worker

    Readings come from the sensors on each worker's strap: BMP180 (air temperature and pressure) and MQ-2 (combustible gas such as LPG, propane, methane and smoke). After a 2-minute warm-up the strap learns clean air, then shows the gas sensor's resistance as a % of clean air: gas makes it drop (yellow at 60 %, red at 35 %). It is relative to clean air, not ppm or % of the explosive limit. Heat levels use the air temperature. This is an early warning, not a certified gas detector.

    Message formats

    Machine → NearStop (UDP port shown under Tools; several times a second):

    {"type":"machine","id":"MA","state":"slowed",
 "dist_cm":87,"zone":"warning","sensor":"ok"}

    dist_cm = -1 when nothing is in range. Optional: "warning_cm"/"danger_cm" (echo of the active thresholds). sensor: ok, no_reading or error.

    NearStop → machine (sent back to the IP:port the reports come from):

    {"type":"set_zones","id":"MA","warning_cm":180,"danger_cm":60}
{"type":"command","id":"MA","cmd":"stop"}   // stop | hold | lock | resume | slow

    Machines & site

    IDMachineX (m)Y (m)Sensor facing (°) Warning (cm)Danger (cm)

    (0, 0) is the bottom-left corner of the site. Facing is the direction the ultrasonic sensor points: 0° = right / east, 90° = up / north, 180° = left / west, 270° = down / south. Defaults: warning 150 cm, danger 50 cm (danger must be smaller). These are the base distances: NearStop raises them automatically when a worker is under strain and sends the new values to the machine.

    Connection

    Machines and the vest connect to NearStop over Wi-Fi: each sends UDP messages to this computer's IP on the UDP port above. Commands go back to the IP:port each machine's messages come from, so machine firmware must send from a fixed port (udp.begin(port)) and listen on it.
